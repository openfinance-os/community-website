Day 1 of Dubai FinTech Summit is over. It was 2 November 2026 at Madinat Jumeirah. The organiser's Day 1 recap, sent the same day, says the programme covered global banking and investment, AI, digital assets, tokenisation and the future of financial markets. Plenary stages are Joharah and Murjan. The innovation stages are Ignyte Arena and MultiBank Group Arena.

The headline themes were AI, tokenisation and digital assets. UAE Open Finance sits under that conversation: customer-authorised data sharing and payments, and a path for agentic access, through one central API Hub. The public directory is the part anyone can check.

## What Day 1 put on the programme

The organiser's recap is the source for the Day 1 themes: global banking and investment, AI, digital assets, tokenisation and the future of financial markets. The same note describes Day 2, today, as the evolving global financial landscape and emerging markets, the next chapter of banking, financial services and digital innovation, digital assets and tokenisation, venture capital and investment, and the FinTech World Cup Grand Finale.

The public programme is on the [Dubai FinTech Summit agenda](https://dubaifintechsummit.com/agenda/), which lists 2 and 3 November at Madinat Jumeirah. This post does not add quotes, speakers, attendance or session outcomes.

## Where the directory stands

The newest Observatory production snapshot is 2 November 2026, 14:47 UTC. **13 of 30** registrations publish an active authorization server. **17** do not. The count is an active authorization server with a nonempty issuer. It is published metadata. Reachability, certification and traffic are separate questions. Source: the public directory at data.directory.openfinance.ae.

The 1 November 2026 snapshot, 12:41 UTC, already recorded 13 of 30. The baseline in our [12 of 30 breakdown](/news/2026-10-29-12-of-30-uae-open-finance-authorization-servers) is older: on 28 October 2026, 12:08 UTC, **12 of 30** published an issuer and **18** did not. Newsletter [issue #1](/newsletter/archive/uae-open-finance-why-12-of-30-registrations) uses that October figure.

Standard Chartered does not publish an authorization server in the 28 October snapshot. From the 29 October snapshot, 12:18 UTC, it does. The 2 November snapshot still records that flag. The change is directory metadata, not a measure of traffic.

UAE Open Finance is supervised by the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE). Banking and insurance services go through a central API Hub. Al Tareq is the brand a customer sees. Nebras operates the Hub under CBUAE oversight. The [FAQ](/learn/faq) separates those roles. In this model the API Hub is the OIDC/FAPI authorization server and token issuer. A third party calls the Hub. The [methodology](/observatory/methodology) is the definition behind the 13 of 30 count.

That is the reading Open Finance OS takes from Day 1's themes. An assistant, a tokenised instrument or a digital-asset service that needs a customer's account still needs a permissioned path. The directory says which registrations publish the authorization server for that path, and which do not.

## Questions worth asking on Day 2

Day 2's programme, as the organiser's recap describes it, includes the next chapter of banking and digital innovation, digital assets and tokenisation, venture capital and investment, and the FinTech World Cup Grand Finale. Four questions follow from the directory, not from a session transcript:

1. Which 13 of the 30 production registrations publish an authorization server, and what does that flag leave untested?
2. If a product moves customer data or a payment, does the journey go through the API Hub?
3. Before an investment conversation treats a registration as live, what sits beside the directory flag?
4. Digital assets and tokenisation are on today's programme. Which of those ideas need a customer's permissioned account data?
